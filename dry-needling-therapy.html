Neuro-Muscular Reset

Deactivating chronic neuromuscular contraction knots with fine filaments.

Dry Needling is an evidence-based modern Western physical therapy technique targeting myofascial trigger points—hyperirritable spots within taut bands of skeletal muscle.

Using micro-thin, solid monofilament needles without medication, our certified clinicians penetrate deep into dysfunctional muscle fibers. The needle stimulates a localized twitch response (LTR), which resets dysfunctional motor endplates, normalizes local biochemical acidity, flushes out inflammatory markers (such as bradykinin and substance P), and immediately restores physiological resting muscle length.

Clinical Indications

Who benefits most from Dry Needling Therapy

Tailored clinical protocols addressing acute limitations, chronic restrictions, and functional rehabilitation goals.

Cervicogenic Headaches & Neck Spasm

Referred tension radiating into the temples and base of the skull from upper trapezius trigger points.

Piriformis Syndrome & Gluteal Pain

Deep buttock trigger points compressing the sciatic nerve pathway, mimicking lumbar disc pain.

Myofascial Back & Quadratus Pain

Deep lower back muscular spasms that restrict twisting, bending, and comfortable sitting.

Rotator Cuff & Shoulder Impingement

Infraspinatus and supraspinatus knots preventing smooth overhead reaching and arm rotation.

Clinical Pathway

How the treatment process works

Structured for meticulous assessment, comfortable delivery, and long-term functional stability.

1. Palpatory Trigger Identification

Precise flat or pincer palpation isolating the taut band and eliciting patient-recognized referred pain.

2. Sterile Skin Preparation

Medical alcohol sanitization and single-use, sterile disposable surgical-grade monofilament needles.

3. Precision Intramuscular Needling

Gentle needle insertion eliciting a brief, therapeutic local twitch response to release the contraction.

4. Passive Elongation & Movement

Immediate post-needling gentle stretching and active mobility drills to cement lengthened muscle memory.

What to Expect in Your Consultation

Your therapist will walk you through the procedure, palpating the exact muscle group before beginning. The needle entry is generally unfelt; when the trigger point releases, a brief dull ache or twitch is experienced. Mild muscular soreness lasting 24 to 48 hours is normal, followed by significant freedom of movement.

Clinical Precautions & Suitability

Dry needling is conducted only after strict clinical screening. It is avoided in areas of active infection, in patients with severe needle phobia, uncontrolled lymphedema, or acute bleeding disorders. Pregnancy considerations require avoidance of specific acupuncture-sensitive trigger points.
