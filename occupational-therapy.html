Mastering the "Occupations" of Childhood

A child's occupation is to play, learn, explore, and become self-reliant.

When sensory processing challenges, motor dyspraxia, or hand weakness interfere with holding a pencil, tying shoelaces, buttoning clothes, or sitting quietly in a classroom, pediatric occupational therapy bridges the gap.

At Aarav Clinics, our Occupational Therapy department is anchored by Dr. Ananya Iyer (BOT / MOT). We operate an expansive sensory integration gym equipped with vestibular swings, climbing structures, tactile texture paths, and weighted resistance zones. Our therapy helps organize children's nervous systems so they can regulate their attention, coordinate their small hand muscles, and thrive in daily school and family life.

Challenges Addressed

Common concerns treated in Occupational Therapy

Sensory Processing Sensitivity

Extreme distress with loud sounds, clothing tags, messy textures, or constant craving for intense spinning and jumping.

Handwriting & Fine Motor Weakness

Awkward pencil grasp, messy writing, hand fatigue when coloring, and difficulty manipulating scissors or blocks.

Self-Care & Daily Living Difficulties

Inability to independently button shirts, manage zippers, brush teeth, or handle eating spoons cleanly.

Motor Planning & Dyspraxia

Clumsiness, frequent bumping into furniture, difficulty coordinating both hands together during play.

Evidence-Guided Approach

How our Occupational Therapists structure care

01

Sensory Profile & Motor Evaluation

Comprehensive standardized evaluation of sensory processing thresholds, hand strength, visual-motor skills, and classroom habits.

02

Vestibular & Proprioceptive Input

Using suspended swings, climbing nets, and deep pressure "heavy work" activities to help children's brains organize sensory input.

03

Intrinsic Hand Muscle Strengthening

Therapy putty games, precision pinch exercises, tweezer play, and adaptive pencil grip training for effortless writing.

04

Sensory Diet & Routine Design

Providing parents and educators with tailored "sensory diets"—calming strategies and movement breaks throughout school and home routines.

Measurable Gains in Child Occupational Therapy

  • Improved Self-Regulation & Focus: Staying seated, attentive, and calm during classroom study and dining.
  • Fluent Handwriting Mechanics: Producing legible letters without wrist pain or premature hand exhaustion.
  • Daily Self-Care Mastery: Proudly dressing, buttoning, putting on shoes, and eating independently.
  • Decreased Meltdowns: Desensitizing touch, sound, and movement sensitivities that trigger fight-or-flight reactions.
